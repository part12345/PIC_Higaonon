01 / The people

Community & identity

The Higaonon are an Indigenous people of Northern Mindanao. Their communities are diverse, and cultural practices can differ between places and families.

Identity & continuity

A living culture, carried forward

Higaonon identity is connected to community, ancestral land, language, customary practices, and relationships with the natural environment. Elders and knowledge holders play important roles in passing on knowledge, while younger generations also shape how heritage continues today.

The name “Higaonon” is often glossed as “people of the living mountains,” but explanations of the name vary by source. This project treats translations and population figures as sourced interpretations—not as universal facts.

02 / History & cultural expressions

History is told from many perspectives

Written studies, community accounts, and oral histories may frame events differently. This overview separates documented research from traditions that should be shared with community guidance.

HISTORY & IDENTITY

Place, memory, and continuity

Academic writing describes Higaonon communities as Indigenous peoples of Northern Mindanao whose histories are connected to ancestral territories, changing settlement patterns, and encounters with outside political and economic forces. A single short website cannot represent every community's history.

Oral histories are living sources of knowledge. Specific origin narratives should be presented with their source, community, and permission—not flattened into one universal story.

Beliefs & practices

Beliefs and ritual practices are diverse and can include knowledge that is restricted or sacred. This project does not reproduce sensitive details without a reliable public source and appropriate community permission.

03 / Cultural heritage

Knowledge, practice & memory

Culture is not a museum object. It is lived, taught, adapted, and sometimes kept within the community.

01

Textiles & handcraft

Published cultural references discuss Higaonon textile traditions, including hinabol and habulan. Materials, patterns, meanings, and who may make or use particular textiles should be described only when a source supports those details.

DSWD Field Office X reference ↗
02

Community & customary life

Community relationships, oral knowledge, customary leadership, and ways of resolving disagreements are important themes in accounts of Indigenous life. Specific ceremonies or restricted knowledge should not be reproduced without reliable documentation and appropriate permission.

Community-led knowledge
03

Materials & livelihood

Local livelihoods may connect with farming, forests, rivers, and handmade materials. These are not identical across all communities; this page avoids claiming that one food, tool, or craft represents every Higaonon person.

Why are some details limited?

When our reviewed sources do not establish a specific practice, we say so rather than invent details or attribute another people’s tradition to the Higaonon.

04 / Language & knowledge

What we preserve—and how

Digital tools can make educational materials easier to find, but they should be used with consent, context, and care.

Aa

Language & oral tradition

Language and oral literature can carry community memory. A 2025 peer-reviewed study discusses digital documentation of the Higaonon language and the need for accessible learning resources. Any story recording should identify its source, language, context, and permissions.

Read the 2025 study ↗
⌁

Land & environment

Place-based knowledge can be sensitive. Public maps should not expose sacred sites, private locations, or boundaries that the community has not authorized for publication.

▣

Traditional technology

Tools and techniques should be documented with specific evidence, community context, and accurate attribution. We do not label a generic basket, loom, or farming tool as uniquely Higaonon without a source.

♡

Consent & ownership

Not all knowledge is meant for public display. Community members should have a voice in what is recorded, how it is presented, and whether it can be reused.

05 / The community today

Living heritage, present realities

Higaonon communities are contemporary communities. Cultural continuity includes learning, work, family life, community leadership, and decisions about what should be shared.

01

Language & education

Research published in 2025 describes efforts to support Higaonon language learning through a culturally responsive mobile dictionary. It also discusses language shift and gaps in accessible learning materials.

Explore the research ↗
02

Livelihood & environment

A regional ethnobotanical study documents plant knowledge in a specific Higaonon community in Mat-I, Claveria. Its findings should not automatically be applied to all communities or used as medical advice.

Read the study ↗
03

Challenges & revitalization

Language documentation, intergenerational transmission, land and resource concerns, and respectful public representation are topics that require current, community-specific evidence. This site presents them as areas for continued research, not as a complete account of every community's situation.
